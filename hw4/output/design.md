# Problem 10 - Styling the Website

Goal: a whimsical site in Yale colors that makes shoppers want to stay and buy.

| What changed | Why it helps customers stick around and buy |
|---|---|
| Yale-blue page (#00356B) with white text, a rounded friendly font (Nunito), and white "islands" for product cards, details, forms, and the chat. | The blue looks unmistakably Yale and feels like the real brand, which builds trust. The white islands keep product photos crisp and text easy to read, so shoppers can actually evaluate the merch. |
| Product cards lift and tilt slightly on hover; buttons and filter chips are rounded pills. | Small, playful feedback makes browsing feel fun and invites shoppers to keep exploring products. |
| Bulldog mascot on the chat that changes mood: thinking while the agent works, happy when it finds products, sad when it can't. | Replies can take 10–30 seconds; a thinking bulldog makes the wait feel charming instead of broken, so shoppers don't give up on the chat. |
| Product cards fly from the chat onto the page when the chat finds items. | Shows clearly that the chat is pulling real products onto the page, drawing the eye to merch shoppers can click and buy. |
| "Welcome back, <name>! Still thinking about this one?" on Home, showing the last product they viewed or one from their chat. | Brings returning shoppers straight back to an item they were already considering, which is the shortest path to a purchase. |
| Paw-print trail follows the mouse; key words get a tilted "sticker" highlight (e.g. "just buy blue!"). | Memorable, on-brand touches that make the site feel playful and give people a reason to linger. |

Design choices that keep it trustworthy:
- No fake urgency (no countdowns or invented "people are viewing this"), and an original bulldog drawing instead of Yale's official logos.
- All animations turn off for visitors who set their device to reduce motion.
